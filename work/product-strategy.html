Artifact 01 Framework

AI Feature Risk Prioritization Framework

Standard prioritization frameworks optimize for impact and effort. They miss a critical third dimension for AI products: the asymmetric cost of being wrong. This framework adds it — and changes what P0 looks like.

Built from: Deloitte HCSC $10M GenAI program Validated at: Signzy AI-powered KYC Format: 4-question framework + 2×2 sequencing matrix

In non-AI product development, a feature that's "good enough" is usually fine. In AI product development, a feature that's right 90% of the time and catastrophically wrong 10% of the time can be worse than no feature at all — especially in regulated industries like healthcare and fintech.

This framework was developed during a $10M GenAI transformation at HCSC (Health Care Service Corporation) and refined during KYC product work at Signzy. It adds one question that standard value/effort matrices miss: what is the downside if the AI gets it wrong?

The Four Questions

Before scoring any AI feature, answer these four questions explicitly — not as a filter, but as weighted inputs to the prioritization score:

Question 01

Upside if right

What is the measurable business value if the AI output is consistently good? Be specific: time saved per interaction, cost reduced per unit, conversion rate improvement. Vague upside is not upside.

Question 02 — The Differentiator

Downside if wrong

What happens when the AI gets it wrong — and it will? Is the failure reversible (agent catches it before it reaches the customer)? Or irreversible (patient acts on bad medical guidance in real time)? The asymmetry matters more than the probability.

Question 03

Data readiness

Does sufficient labeled training data exist to make this AI feature reliable at the accuracy threshold required? "We can get it" is not "it exists." Features with data gaps move to later phases regardless of upside.

Question 04

Measurability

Can you measure whether this AI feature is actually working once deployed? If you can't instrument success, you can't iterate. Unmeasurable AI features are not product features — they're experiments with no feedback loop.

The 2×2 Sequencing Matrix

Map each proposed AI feature against two axes: Upside if right (business value when AI performs well) and Downside if wrong (consequence severity when AI fails). This produces four quadrants with distinct sequencing rules:

← Downside if Wrong (Low to High)
Upside if Right →
Quadrant A — Avoid Early

High Downside · Low Upside

Worst quadrant. High risk of harm with limited benefit. These features need either a containment design (human-in-the-loop, easy override) or should not be built until the upside case is clearer.

Autonomous medical advice Real-time compliance decisions
Quadrant B — Phase 2+

High Downside · High Upside

Flagship features. Maximum value but maximum risk. Build these after you have delivery credibility from Phase 1. Use Phase 1 success to earn the right to deploy these with stakeholder trust.

Real-time agent guidance Autonomous loan decisions
Quadrant C — Phase 1 Priority

Low Downside · Low Upside

Easy wins that build AI product literacy in the organization and prove the team can ship AI reliably. Don't underestimate these — they create the culture of AI adoption before the stakes are high.

Meeting summary generation First-draft release notes
Quadrant D — P0 Targets

Low Downside · High Upside

The sweet spot. High business value, low failure risk because a human reviews the output before it reaches the customer or system. Ship these first. Use them to build delivery credibility for Quadrant B.

Smart email drafting Post-call documentation KYC document quality feedback
The Sequencing Rule

Ship Quadrant D first. Use it to earn Quadrant B.

1

Phase 1: Identify all Quadrant D features (High Upside, Low Downside). These are your proof-of-concept candidates. Ship them reliably.

2

Phase 1 → 2 Gate: Measure adoption and performance from Phase 1. A 70%+ adoption rate or clear business impact metric is your proof point for Phase 2 expansion.

3

Phase 2: Now deploy Quadrant B features — with stakeholder trust established, containment design proven, and data readiness validated by Phase 1 usage.

4

Ongoing: Quadrant A features require explicit containment design — human-in-the-loop review, easy override, confidence scores — before they leave Phase 2.

Applied: HCSC GenAI Contact Center Program

QA sentiment flagging Coverage from <10% to 80%+ Medium — false positives waste supervisor time B Phase 1 · P1

Result: Smart email drafting shipped in Sprint 4 with a 78% acceptance rate. That number became the proof point that unlocked Phase 2 — including real-time guidance, which was the feature everyone wanted from day one.

Artifact 02 Strategy Brief

India Fintech — A PM's Product Strategy Brief

Three strategic bets for building in India's credit, payments, and financial infrastructure landscape. Written from the perspective of a PM who has shipped in Indian fintech and watched the market evolve in real time.

Context: India · 2025–2027 Informed by: Signzy, JPMC strategic analysis, RBI regulatory tracking Format: Market signals + 3 strategic bets + PM implications

India's fintech market is at an inflection point. UPI has democratized payments. Digital lending has scaled. But three structural gaps remain — and they represent the most interesting product opportunities for PMs building in this space over the next 2–3 years.

The Market Signals Worth Tracking

Credit Gap
₹22T
Estimated MSME credit gap in India. Most small businesses cannot access formal credit despite having transaction history. The problem is underwriting, not product.
AA Adoption
5M+
Account Aggregator users linked as of 2025. India's AA framework is a legitimate data infrastructure breakthrough — most PMs haven't built products on top of it yet.
UPI Merchant Gap
~80M
Small merchants accepting UPI payments. Almost none have a working capital product tied to their UPI transaction data. The rails exist. The product doesn't.

Three Strategic Bets

1
Bet 01 · Credit Infrastructure
Build the underwriting layer, not the lending product.

The India credit gap isn't a capital problem — banks have capital. It's an underwriting problem. Lenders don't have reliable cash flow data for MSMEs who live outside the formal banking system but inside the UPI ecosystem.

The strategic bet: build a cash-flow-based credit underwriting API that consumes UPI transaction data (via Account Aggregator) and produces a risk score that lenders can trust. This is infrastructure, not a consumer product — and infrastructure compounds. Once a lender integrates your underwriting API, switching cost is high and coverage expands with every new data partner.

Why this is the right bet: RBI's Account Aggregator framework created a legal structure for data sharing that didn't exist before. The PM who understands AA consent flows and can translate them into credit risk models is rare — and valuable.

Account AggregatorB2B APIMSME creditRBI-compliant
2
Bet 02 · Working Capital at the Point of Sale
The small merchant with UPI rails already has the data for a working capital product. Nobody's built it yet.

80M+ small merchants in India accept UPI payments. Every transaction is a data point: daily revenue, seasonality, customer retention, growth trajectory. This is the cleanest cash flow signal in the market — and it's sitting in Razorpay, PhonePe, and Paytm's databases being used only for payment analytics.

The strategic bet: offer working capital (₹1L–₹10L) to merchants with 6+ months of UPI transaction history, underwritten entirely on transaction data, with repayment automatically deducted from incoming UPI payments. No collateral, no financial statements, no branch visit.

Why this works as a product: The merchant's transaction data is both the underwriting input and the repayment mechanism. The product is self-collateralizing by design. CAC is near-zero if you're already the payment provider. This is the same playbook as Shopify Capital — applied to UPI infrastructure.

Embedded financeUPI dataMSME lendingRevenue-based repayment
3
Bet 03 · AI-Native KYC for the Next 300M
The next wave of Indian bank customers don't have documents that match current KYC systems. The product needs to meet them where they are.

India's existing KYC infrastructure was designed for urban professionals with Aadhaar, PAN, and a stable address. The next 300M financial inclusion targets are semi-urban and rural users with inconsistent documentation, shared addresses, and intermittent connectivity.

The strategic bet: an AI-native KYC stack that treats documentation as a probabilistic signal, not a binary gate. Face-match with liveness as the primary identifier. AA-linked financial history as a supplementary signal. Alternative address verification through telecom data and utility payments.

The PM insight from Signzy: The hardest part of this problem is not the AI model — it's designing the failure experience for users who fail the first time. First-attempt success rate is the north star. Guided capture and real-time feedback (not post-hoc rejection) moves that number more than any model improvement.

Financial inclusionLiveness detectionRBI complianceTier-2/3 India

PM Implication Across All Three Bets

Each of these bets has RBI compliance as a first-class design constraint, not a post-launch checkbox. The PMs who win in Indian fintech are the ones who treat regulatory knowledge as a product skill — because it determines what you can build, how fast, and for whom.

Artifact 03 Product Roadmap

Enterprise Insurance Platform — 18-Month Product Strategy

What a phased product strategy looks like for an enterprise insurance SaaS serving tier-1 carriers. Informed by current work at Sapiens, sanitized and generalized for portfolio use.

Context: Enterprise Insurtech · B2B SaaS Informed by: Sapiens CoreSuite (sanitized) Format: 3-phase strategy + success metrics + phase-gate criteria

Enterprise insurance platforms face a distinctive strategic challenge: the platform serves multiple carrier clients simultaneously, each with different digital maturity levels, different integration architectures, and different competitive pressures. A single roadmap must advance platform differentiation while serving the renewal and expansion needs of clients at different stages.

This strategy organizes the 18-month investment into three phases, each with a different strategic priority and a clear phase-gate criterion before moving to the next.

1
Months 1–6 · Foundation
Reliability as a Product — Earn the Trust That Unlocks Expansion

Before any growth feature, clients need to trust the platform will do what it says it will. The first phase focuses on reliability, integration stability, and the communication cadence that makes carriers comfortable expanding their usage.

Bet 1A
API contract stability
Zero breaking changes to existing integrations. Version all APIs. Carriers plan around our releases — make that planning trustworthy.
Bet 1B
Pre-production quality gate
95% quality standard before UAT opens. Insurance carriers cannot absorb production defects. Reliability is the foundation everything else builds on.
Bet 1C
Release predictability
Consistent, template-based release communication. Carriers need to plan their own digital roadmaps around ours. Predictability is a feature.

Phase Gate: Zero P1 defects shipped in Phase 1. Client satisfaction score stable or improving. At least 2 carriers expand their integration scope after Phase 1 closes.

2
Months 7–12 · Self-Service
Digital Self-Service — Reduce Carrier Ops Burden and Increase Policyholder Engagement

With trust established, the second phase builds the customer-facing capabilities that reduce carrier operational cost and create visible product improvement for policyholders. This is where platform differentiation becomes visible.

Bet 2A
Policyholder digital portal
Self-service policy management, fund selection, and digital claims initiation. Target: 30% reduction in inbound carrier support calls from policyholders.
Bet 2B
Fund management UX
Simplified digital disclosure and interactive fund selection for complex insurance investment products. Reduce transaction abandonment during fund switching workflows.
Bet 2C
Analytics dashboard for carriers
Real-time policyholder funnel visibility for carrier ops teams. Same insight we provided to Signzy's bank clients — carriers should see their own data.

Phase Gate: Digital portal live for at least 2 carrier clients. Measurable reduction in support ticket volume. Net Promoter Score for carrier IT teams stable or improving.

3
Months 13–18 · Intelligence
AI-Assisted Platform — From Transaction Processing to Decision Support

With reliability proven and self-service live, the third phase introduces AI capabilities that transform the platform from a transaction processor into a decision-support system for both carriers and policyholders.

Bet 3A
AI-assisted claims triage
Classify incoming claims by complexity and risk. Route complex claims to senior adjusters, simple claims to automated processing. Apply the AI Risk Framework — low downside first.
Bet 3B
Proactive renewal intelligence
ML model predicting policyholder churn risk 90 days before renewal. Surfaces high-risk accounts to carrier retention teams with recommended intervention actions.
Bet 3C
Personalized policyholder nudges
AI-generated, carrier-branded communications for fund rebalancing reminders, coverage gap alerts, and renewal prompts. Personalized by life stage and portfolio.

Phase Gate: AI triage model in production with measurable accuracy. Carrier retention teams actively using churn predictions. Platform positioned as "intelligent" in carrier renewal conversations.

Success Metrics Across Phases

P1 Defect Rate

The Strategic Logic Across All Three Phases

Each phase earns the right to the next. Phase 1's reliability unlocks Phase 2's self-service expansion. Phase 2's data (policyholder behavior in the portal) becomes the training signal for Phase 3's AI features. The sequence is not arbitrary — it's the only order that manages risk while building platform value.
