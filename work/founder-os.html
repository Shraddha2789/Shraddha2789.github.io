Problem Statement
What breaks and why it matters

Current state: A typical early-stage founder runs 6+ disconnected tools simultaneously — Notion for docs and OKRs, Linear for product, Airtable for investor CRM, Google Sheets for burn rate, Greenhouse for hiring, Slack for everything else. None of these talk to each other.

The real problem isn't tool fragmentation. It's context fragmentation. Founders make bad decisions not because they lack data — but because the relevant context lives in a different tab. You're reviewing a hiring decision without knowing you're 3 months from runway. You're pitching a new feature roadmap without knowing your lead investor privately hated the last one. You're setting Q3 OKRs without realising two of your key hires are stuck in an offer negotiation that's been cold for 2 weeks.

Root cause: Generic productivity tools (Notion, Linear, Airtable) were built for teams doing one type of work. A founder's work is fundamentally cross-domain — every decision about product affects hiring affects investor sentiment affects burn. No single tool models this, so context leaks between apps and founders make decisions with partial information.

Existing alternatives and their failure modes:

  • Notion: Great for docs; no structured tracking, no signals, no cross-domain awareness
  • Linear: Excellent for eng teams; too engineering-centric for founder-level context
  • Airtable: Flexible but requires constant configuration; not opinionated enough for investor CRM
  • Visible.vc / Carta: Investor reporting focused; doesn't connect to operational decisions
  • Comprehensive ERPs (SAP, Oracle): Built for enterprises; wrong scale, wrong abstractions, wrong price point

Product Vision
What Founder OS is and isn't

Vision statement: Founder OS is the decision layer for early-stage companies — an operating system that connects the five core domains of a founder's job (goals, capital, people, product, investors) into one workspace and surfaces the cross-domain context that matters before you need to go looking for it.

What it is
  • A unified workspace for founder-level decision-making
  • A context engine — surfaces cross-domain signals proactively
  • Opinionated structure built around the founder's job, not generic project management
  • Built for the 0→1 phase: pre-seed through Series A
What it is not
  • Not another general project management tool
  • Not an accounting or CFO tool (integrates, doesn't replace)
  • Not a co-founder or team OS (founder-first, team features secondary)
  • Not an analytics dashboard (action-oriented, not report-oriented)
The North Star

A founder should be able to open Founder OS at the start of any week and immediately know: what is at risk, what is the one thing that moves the needle, and what context they need before their next important conversation. No digging, no switching, no assembly required.

Target Personas
Who we're building for and in what priority order
Primary · The Solo Founder

Pre-seed, wearing every hat. Needs ruthless prioritisation above all else — too many decisions, not enough time. Primary job-to-be-done: "Tell me what matters today."

Primary · The Founding Team

2–4 co-founders, early hires. Needs shared context and role clarity — who owns what, and what is the single version of truth on runway, priorities, and pipeline.

Secondary · The VC-backed CEO

Post-seed, board obligations. Needs investor reporting artifacts to be produced automatically from operational data — not hand-assembled from 6 tools the night before a board meeting.

Out of scope for v1: Series B+ companies (different complexity), solo freelancers (different structure), non-tech founders (different tool fluency baseline).

The Differentiator — Context Engine
What makes Founder OS different from a collection of good templates

The core insight: Individual modules aren't the product. The product is the connections between them. Every competing tool does one domain well. Founder OS is defined by what happens at the intersections.

1
Hiring ↔ Runway When you add a candidate to the final interview stage, Founder OS automatically recalculates runway assuming that offer gets accepted at the expected salary. You see the runway impact before you make the hire — not after payroll runs.
2
OKR ↔ Product ↔ Hiring If a Q3 OKR is at risk, Founder OS surfaces which product initiatives are blocking it and which open roles are blocking those initiatives. You see the causal chain, not just a red status badge.
3
Investor ↔ Roadmap Before your next investor check-in, Founder OS reminds you of their stated areas of concern (logged in your last CRM note) and surfaces whether any roadmap changes since then address or conflict with those concerns.
4
Board Update Auto-Draft Board updates are assembled from operational data — OKR progress, burn vs. plan, hiring progress, key risks — and presented as a structured first draft. No more spending 3 hours copying numbers from 5 tabs into a slide deck the night before.

Phased Roadmap
What ships when and why in that order
1
Phase 1 — The Core OS (Q3 2026 target) Mission Control dashboard, OKR Engine, Burn Intelligence, and Investor CRM. These four modules alone replace four separate tools and establish the cross-domain context layer. Success metric: a founder can open Founder OS Monday morning and know their top 3 priorities without switching apps.
2
Phase 2 — Talent & Product (Q4 2026) Talent Pipeline and Product Compass modules, plus the first version of cross-domain signals (Hiring ↔ Runway, OKR ↔ Product ↔ Hiring). Requires Phase 1 data model to be stable — sequenced deliberately so cross-domain features have real data to connect.
3
Phase 3 — Intelligence Layer (Q1 2027) AI-assisted decision support: board update auto-drafts, investor communication summaries, OKR-at-risk early warnings, and proactive runway alerts. Phase 3 is only credible if Phase 1 and 2 have established data quality and user trust — AI on bad data destroys trust faster than no AI at all.

Risk Register
Known risks, severity, and mitigations
RiskSeverityMitigation
Scope creep to "team OS" — user requests pull toward team features before founder-core is solid High Hard product boundary: no team collaboration features in Phase 1. Revisit after 100 active founder users.
Founder context-switching inertia — existing Notion/Linear setups are deeply customized; switching cost is real High Import-first strategy: structured importers for Notion, Airtable, and Google Sheets on day one. Meet founders where they are.
Cross-domain signals feel like noise — too many automated connections become alert fatigue Medium Weekly digest format (not real-time push) for Phase 1. Validate signal relevance with 20 beta founders before expanding.
Financial data sensitivity — burn rate and runway are sensitive; founders hesitant to enter real numbers Medium SOC 2 Type II roadmap from day one. Offer local-first data option for MVP cohort. Transparent data model documentation.
AI draft quality in Phase 3 — board update drafts with hallucinated metrics destroy trust immediately Medium Phase 3 AI features are gated behind Phase 1/2 data completeness scores. No draft generated unless source data confidence is high.
Narrow ICP — very specific to pre-seed/seed stage; TAM is real but bounded Low Accepted trade-off. Depth of fit for 0→1 founders beats breadth. Series A+ expansion is a Phase 4 decision, not a Phase 1 constraint.

Open Questions — Discovery in Progress
What I'm still working to validate before committing to the Phase 1 spec
  • Onboarding depth vs. time-to-value: How much context does a founder need to input before Mission Control becomes useful? Too much setup and they churn before they see value. Current hypothesis: 15-minute structured onboarding unlocks 80% of Phase 1 value — validating this with 10 founder interviews.
  • Investor CRM network effects: Does the product get more valuable if multiple founders share warm intros data? Or does that privacy concern kill adoption before it starts? Leaning toward opt-in, single-founder default.
  • Pricing model: Flat SaaS ($49/mo) vs. per-seat vs. usage-based. Founders are cost-conscious pre-revenue; bootstrapped vs. VC-backed founders have very different willingness-to-pay. Hypothesis: flat $49/mo, free for pre-revenue with YC/founder accelerator partnerships.
  • Integration vs. native data entry: Should Founder OS integrate with QuickBooks/Stripe for burn data, or require manual entry? Integrations increase trust and accuracy but add engineering complexity and create dependency risk. Phase 1 decision: manual entry with CSV import; Phase 2 integrations.
