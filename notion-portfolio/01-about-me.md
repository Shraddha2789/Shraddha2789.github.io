## How I Work

**I start with the user, not the feature.**
Before writing a single user story, I want to understand: who is this for, what are they actually trying to do, and what's in their way? I use customer interviews, usage analytics, and direct client feedback — not gut instinct and opinion.

**I make the prioritization process visible.**
Backlog grooming shouldn't be a black box. I use scoring frameworks (RICE, MoSCoW, value vs. effort matrices) and I make the tradeoffs explicit to stakeholders before committing to a roadmap. This avoids surprises at sprint review and builds trust with engineering.

**I treat engineering as a partner, not a vendor.**
The best product decisions I've made came from conversations with engineers who said "there's a smarter way to do this." I stay close to technical constraints — not because I'm trying to be an engineer, but because technical feasibility is a product constraint.

**I write specs that engineers actually want to read.**
Clear acceptance criteria, explicit edge cases, annotated wireframes. A vague spec is a tax on engineering velocity.

**I don't confuse output with outcome.**
Shipped features are inputs. What matters is whether they moved the metric that mattered. I track post-launch performance obsessively and I'm comfortable killing features that didn't work.
